He promptly and strongly rejects Daendels's principles and actions, getting along best with those who had been Daendels's enemies. Although Daendels left him a well-established military force, the British were undoubtedly stronger, better organized, and better supplied. |
| **Aug. 1811:** On Disembarkation of Brit. packhouses in Bat. [Batavia] + coffee / sugar / spices burnt by J. [Janssens].<br>Great mistake: (a) Brit. have great supplies of col. products ∴ makes no difference to them; (b) many of products had been promised to individuals who had lent money to the govt.<br>`*` Brit. put out dirct confiscate products as Booty. | **August 1811:** Upon the British disembarkation, warehouses in Batavia containing coffee, sugar, and spices were burned by Janssens—a major blunder because (a) the British already possessed abundant colonial produce, so the loss made no difference to them, and (b) much of that produce had been pledged to individuals who had lent money to the government. The British immediately moved to confiscate remaining products as booty. |
