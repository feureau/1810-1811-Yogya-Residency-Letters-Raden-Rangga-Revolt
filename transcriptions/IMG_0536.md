## Metadata

**Archival Reference:** ANRI *Djokjo Brieven* no. 27  
**Date:** 1810-07-02 to 1810-07-07 (Javanese: 28 Jumadilawal 1737 [2 July 1810] to 5 Jumadilakir 1737 [7 July 1810]; Carey header date: `2 July – 13 July 1810`)  
**Key Events:**
* Daendels' Treaty Demands on Military Deserters (28 Jumadilawal 1737 [2 July 1810] / Xerox I): Governor-General Herman Willem Daendels, writing through Resident Johannes Wilhelmus Moorrees to Sultan HB II [Hamengkubuwana II], reports that large numbers of former VOC and colonial auxiliary troops (Javanese, Malays, Balinese, Madurese, Macassarese, Bugis, Dayak) have deserted and found refuge within the royal territories of Yogyakarta and Surakarta.
* Invoking the *Sêrat Anggèr* (the 1792 Treaty between the VOC and HB II), Daendels orders the Sultan to extradite all runaway soldiers immediately. He notes they are easily identified by their short-cropped military hair, and requests that a synchronized dragnet be executed across all villages on the very same day to prevent them from fleeing between districts, urging HB II to dispatch royal warrants to his palace retainers (*abdi-dalem*).
* Solo River Teak Flotilla and Boatbuilding Directive (DR II to Moorrees, 5 Jumadilakir 1737 [7 July 1810]):
  - Daendels instructs Moorrees to oversee the construction of small transport boats on the Solo River to haul teak timber from royal forests (Grobogan, Tanggulangin, Demak) to the coast.
  - Moorrees transmits the order to Patih DR II [Radèn Adipati Danureja II] and Radèn Rongga Prawiradirja III, directing eastern mancanagara bupatis to build craft within their regencies.
  - DR II is instructed to select suitable timber tracts and staging depots facilitating direct transport downriver to the northern coastal ports (*Pasisir*).
  - Chinese and Pasisir artisans: Chinese boatwrights and coastal specialists are to be permitted to occupy a designated village under strict regulatory supervision exclusively for boatbuilding.
  - The agreement restricts commercial timber concessions: retail sales of timber along the northern coast are strictly regulated, and unlicensed timber harvesting from royal reserves is strictly prohibited.
