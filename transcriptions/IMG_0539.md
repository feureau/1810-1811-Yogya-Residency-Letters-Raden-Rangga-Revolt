# Transcription of IMG_0539_rotated_90.jpg

## Metadata
**Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0539_rotated_90.jpg`  
**Archival Reference:** ANRI *Djokjo Brieven* no. 27  
**Date:** 1810-08-19 to 1810-09-10 (19 August 1810 / Oogstmaand to 10 September 1810 / Herfstmaand; citing treaty of 26 October 1808)  
**Key Events:**
* Evasion of the Japan Inquest (Moorrees to Daendels, 19 August 1810 / Oogstmaand): Sultan HB II [Hamengkubuwana II] replies to Governor-General Herman Willem Daendels' dispatch of 8 August, but maintains complete silence regarding the demand to surrender the three Patihs of Japan [Mojokerto]. The full facts remain obscured; a Javanese scribal appendix is addressed to court interpreter (*jurubasa*) Djasiro.
* Chinese Fiscal Status and Legal Protection (Moorrees to Daendels, 22 August 1810): Citing Daendels' decree of 18 Grasmaand [April 1810], Chinese residents are formally authorized to collect head money (*hoofdgeld*) and placed under the explicit protection of the colonial government. Missives handed over to incoming Resident Pieter Engelhard on 1 September 1810.
* Border Disarmament and Forest Security (Engelhard to Leonard van Pabst, Director of Forests at Rembang, 5 Herfstmaand [September] 1810, Yogya):
  - Engelhard recalls their warm personal friendship, hoping to meet Van Pabst at Gunung Sari.
  - Border security decree: No Yogyakarta subject may cross provincial frontiers armed with rifles, gunpowder, or lead ammunition without an official government passport.
  - Engelhard invokes the foundational Border Accord of 26 October 1808 negotiated between the Patihs of Yogyakarta and Surakarta (DR II [Radèn Adipati Danureja II] and Radèn Adipati Danuningrat), drafted under the joint supervision of Jacob Andries van Braam and Engelhard.
  - To thwart scheming village chiefs (*intriguing desa heads*), village boundary defense protocols require the rhythmic sounding of wooden rice mortars (*sounding of rice blocks*) as an immediate frontier alarm.
* Demak Highway Robbery on Chinese Merchant (Goldbach, Landdrost of Semarang and Demak, to Engelhard, 10 September 1810 / Herfstmaand):
  - In the dead of night, a heavily armed predatory gang raided Demak, plundering a prominent Chinese merchant/tax-farmer (*bandhar*) of merchandise and silver valued at 10,000 Spanish Dollars [Sp. D.].
  - Four bandits were shot dead (identified as local Demak Javanese); one wounded prisoner was captured, who confessed under interrogation that the raiding cadre originated from Gabus in the Wirasari district.
