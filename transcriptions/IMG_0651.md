### Two-Column Transcription (Margin Notes / Javanese Script vs. Main Text / Dutch Translation)

| Margin Notes / Javanese Script | Main Text / Dutch Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Paulus to Servatius**<br>9 Dec. 1810<br>(Maospati)<br><br>*Coastal Roadblocks* | Lieutenant Thomas Paulus to Secretary Servatius (9 Dec. 1810, from Maospati):<br>• Radèn Tumênggung Sasradiningrat [SD] in Rajêgwêsi writes to Purwadipura [PWD], confirming receipt of HB II's direct royal mandate to assault Rangga and execute him.<br>• Sasradiningrat resolves to march and join Purwadipura.<br>• SD issues strict orders to the regents of Sekaran, Bauwêrna, and Panolan [highlighted in orange] to place heavy cordons over all forest trails and roads, preventing Rangga from escaping north to the coast (*Pasisir*).<br>• Purwadipura orders Sasradiningrat to convey the wife, household, and retainers of Mas Tumênggung Sumanêgara of Padagangan to Madiun for safe custody.<br>• Three regents — Sasrakusuma, Sastranagara, and Yudakusuma [or Sudakusuma] — depart Madiun to search for Rangga's mother and children, reported hiding close by. |
| **Rangga at Bèrbèk**<br>9 Dec. 1810<br><br>*(Red underline: Bèrbèk)* | Rebel disintegration and movements:<br>• A deserter from the rebel column confirms that Rangga is wandering aimlessly from village to village with only 50 to 60 remaining followers, mass desertions having shattered his ranks.<br>• Rangga is presently located at **Bèrbèk** in Yogyakarta royal territory, having previously crossed through the Nala district of Caruban.<br>• Commanders anticipate closing the encirclement around Rangga shortly. |
| **Eng. to Servatius**<br>12 Dec. 1810<br><br>*Defense of HB II* | Resident Pieter Engelhard [Eng.] to Servatius (12 Dec. 1810):<br>• Reaffirms his firm conviction that HB II [Sultan Hamengkubuwana II] had no part in Rangga's treasonous conspiracy, having consistently demonstrated royal indignation against him.<br>• Convinced Daendels will recognize the Sultan's sincerity upon witnessing his extraordinary concessions (*toegeeflijkheid*), having delivered up Natakusuma and Natadiningrat as hostages until the rebellion ends.<br>• Radèn Tumênggung Natapraja [NP] is formally pardoned by Daendels.<br>• Natakusuma and Natadiningrat scheduled to march for Semarang on Saturday (c. 14 Dec. 1810). |
| **Ratu Anom's Scene**<br>Dec. 1810<br><br>*(Marked with red vertical rule)* | **Pathos of Dynastic Separation:**<br>• HB II is profoundly shaken and visibly moved at Natadiningrat's departure — not out of apprehension for Natadiningrat's personal safety, but on account of his beloved daughter, Radèn Ayu Anom (daughter of Queen Ratu Kencana Wulan).<br>• Radèn Ayu Anom cast herself weepingly at Sultan HB II's feet, imploring his royal compassion and protection.<br>• The Sultan is described as being deeply affected by her despair.<br>• Court observers express acute anxiety that Daendels has been utterly duped by malicious Surakarta slander (? Sunan Pakubuwana IV). |
