| **Raja Putra Narendra [Sultan Hamengkubuwana III] to Eng. [Pieter Engelhard], 4 May 1811:**<br>Daendels [Marshal Herman Willem Daendels] informed of state of affairs in Yogya by Sala Court.<br>Asks RPN [Raja Putra Naréndra] to discuss matter + HB II [Sultan Hamengkubuwana II] + warn him that Daendels would again march on Yogya.<br>RPN denies that prayers / *prajurits* any sinister meaning. Prayers common at *Mulud* Birthday of Prophet.<br>HB II hoping that if British attack Java:<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• NK [Pangéran Natakusuma] would be allowed back to Yogya.<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• + wants to kill RPN either by Dagger or by poison + followers.<br>`∴ Existence of RPN depends entirely on Dutch control of Java.` | **Raja Putra Narendra to Pieter Engelhard, 4 May 1811:**<br>Daendels was informed of Yogyakarta affairs by the Surakarta Court, and asks Raja Putra Narendra to discuss the matter with Hamengkubuwana II and warn him that Daendels would march on Yogyakarta again.<br>Raja Putra Narendra denies any sinister intent behind the prayers or *prajurits*, noting that prayers are customary at *Mulud* (the Prophet's birthday). However, Hamengkubuwana II is hoping that if the British attack Java, Natakusuma will be allowed back to Yogyakarta, and wishes to kill Raja Putra Narendra (and his followers) by dagger or poison.<br>*Conclusion:* Raja Putra Narendra's survival depends entirely on Dutch control of Java. |
| **DR II [Radèn Adipati Danureja II] to Eng., 4 May 1811 / 10 Rabingulakir 1738:**<br>Daendels not satisfied + DR II's Behaviour. Too weak = still under influence of HB II.<br>Had not told Eng. that had visited HB II.<br>DR II tells Eng. to take news coming from Sala + a pinch of salt. | **Danureja II to Pieter Engelhard, 4 May 1811 (10 Rabingulakir AJ 1738):**<br>Daendels is dissatisfied with Danureja II's conduct, viewing him as too weak and still under Hamengkubuwana II's influence—having failed to tell Engelhard that he had visited Hamengkubuwana II. Danureja II advises Engelhard to take news coming from Surakarta with a pinch of salt. |
| **DR II to Eng., 13 Rabingulakir / 6 May 1811:**<br>News from Waterloo in Cirêbon 30 Apr. 1811 that NK / ND [Radèn Tumenggung Natadiningrat] still there.<br>`∴ Rumour that ND back in Yogya false.`<br>But Wat. [Matthijs Waterloo] discovers writing materials amongst NK / ND's Baggage.<br><span style="color:#a00">||</span> `∴ Wat. splits NK / ND apart.` Jailed in separate cells + separated from 5 women from Yogya who had been looking after them.<br>NK / ND v. high fever (malaria).<br>1 Chinese amongst 5 female servants of NK / ND.<br>DR II says Dangerous if 5 female servants allowed back to Yogya. Should be kept under close watch in Cirêbon. | **Danureja II to Pieter Engelhard, 6 May 1811 (13 Rabingulakir AJ 1738):**<br>News from Waterloo in Cirebon (30 April 1811) confirms Natakusuma and Natadiningrat are still there, proving the rumor of Natadiningrat's return to Yogyakarta false. However, Waterloo discovered writing materials in their baggage, and therefore separated Natakusuma and Natadiningrat into separate cells and parted them from the five women from Yogyakarta who had been tending them.<br>Both prisoners suffer from severe fever (malaria). One Chinese woman is among the five female servants. Danureja II warns it would be dangerous to allow those servants back to Yogyakarta; they should remain under close watch in Cirebon. |
