# Transcription of IMG_0531_rotated_90.jpg

## Metadata
**Image File:** `jpg/IMG_0531_rotated_90.jpg`  
**Archival Reference:** ANRI *Djokjo Brieven* no. 46 (R. Rangga 1810–1811)  
**Date:** 1810-07-06 to 1810-07-09 (6 Hooimaand to 9 Hooimaand [July] 1810)  
**Key Events:**
* HB II [Sultan Hamengkubuwana II] denounces Patih DR II [Radèn Adipati Danureja II], contrasting his subservience to the Dutch with the stalwart conduct of his grandfather, DR I [Radèn Adipati Danureja I, first Patih of Yogya]. When DR II begs royal forgiveness, the Sultan retorts that his faults are already inscribed permanently on paper.
* Resident Johannes Wilhelmus Moorrees consoles DR II in the presence of Radèn Rongga Prawiradirja III, advising him that he has nothing to reproach himself for regarding the Sultan, and promises to make a comprehensive report to Governor-General Herman Willem Daendels if HB II attempts to suspend or remove him.
* The Footstool (*Voetbankje*) Crisis: Moorrees reports to Daendels (6–9 July 1810) that HB II has given no further official reply since 2 July. Moorrees insists that if the Sultan wishes to receive dispatches from Sunan Pakubuwana IV, he must submit unreservedly to Daendels' 1808 Ceremonial Regulations.
* DR II reports that while HB II might agree to compromise on certain ceremonial courtesies, he adamantly refuses to abolish the royal footstool (*voetbankje*) beneath his throne, which ensures he sits conspicuously elevated above the Dutch Resident.
* HB II argues historical precedent: he had used the footstool during the residencies of Buyskes and during Daendels' own state visit, and considers the acquiescence of Governor-General Albertus Henricus Wiese and Pieter Engelhard as confirmed customary recognition (*adat*).
* Moorrees confronts DR II, Radèn Tumenggung Natadiningrat, and Radèn Tumenggung Danukusuma I, declaring Daendels' orders regarding the footstool unchangeable; if HB II persists, Moorrees is commanded to evacuate Yogya, placing full responsibility for war on the Sultan's head.
* Natadiningrat and Danukusuma confess that there is not the slightest ray of hope of dissuading the Sultan.
