| **p. 418:** Recounts the British landing at Batavia Bay on 4 August 1811, fall of Meester Cornelis on 26 August, and Janssens's [Lieutenant-General Jan Willem Janssens] Capitulation of Kali Tuntang on 18 September, under which Dutch (or French) civil officials were required to continue serving until replaced by British appointees. Notes pro-English sympathies among "old-constitution" Dutchmen, contrasted with officials sworn to Emperor Napoleon. | **Page 418:** Summarizes the British invasion (4 Aug. – 18 Sept. 1811), the transitional clause in the Kali Tuntang Capitulation keeping Dutch civil servants at their posts, and the conflicting loyalties between pro-British "old-constitution" Dutch officials and those sworn to Napoleon. |
| **p. 419:** Cites Janssens's decree of 2 August 1811 (*Plakaatboek*, XVI, p. 725) ordering all civil officials to stay three hours behind the retreating army on pain of court-martial and death, and his 10 August letter to the Landdrost of Batavia reassuring officials that General Auchmuty would not confiscate property of those obeying orders and exhorting fidelity to their oath. | **Page 419:** Cites Janssens's 2 August 1811 evacuation order (*Plakaatboek* XVI, p. 725) and his 10 August circular to the Landdrost of Batavia countering rumors of British property confiscation and reminding officials of their solemn oath. |
