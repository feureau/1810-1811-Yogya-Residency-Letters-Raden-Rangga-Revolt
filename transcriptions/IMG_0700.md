Gen. | Janssens was an officer's son who received no proper education in his youth. Strongly attached to the House of Orange, he became a Captain during the 1793–1794 campaigns, attached to Prince Frederick's division, and was wounded. In 1795 he served in the Commissariat overseeing the provisioning of French troops in Holland, maintaining good relations with both French and Dutch authorities. After the 1802 Peace of Amiens, he was rewarded with the Governorship of the Cape Colony and the rank of Lieutenant-General. |
| Shows *Eerlijkheid* / High moral character. But hesitant rule of civilian pop. / Admin.<br>Little knowledge of men. V. passionate spirit / makes many people unlucky. Own son — commits suicide.<br>In Defense of Colony v. English looses his head — although mil. aspects well worked out. Court-martialed on his return, he was acquitted/pardoned, though his incompetence (*onbekwaamheid*) was censured. King Louis appointed him Secretary of the Ministry of War and Intendant-General of the *Corps d'Armée*, taking the field in 1806. |
