## Annotated Transcription

| Original Text & Annotations | English Translation / Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **p. 420:** Continuation of Janssens's [Lieutenant-General Jan Willem Janssens] 10 August letter promising indemnification for loyal officials. Describes the case of **M.W. Keuchenius**, member of the High Court of Justice (*Hoogen Raad van Justitie*) in Batavia, who crossed into British-occupied territory before Meester Cornelis fell and was declared a "traitor, perjurer, dishonorable and infamous" in absentia. *(Margin note: "M.W. Keuchenius first to go over to Brit. controlled Territory")* | **Page 420:** Details the case of **M.W. Keuchenius**, the first High Court judge to cross into British-held territory, whom Janssens's government stripped of office and condemned in absentia as a perjurer prior to the fall of Meester Cornelis. |
| **p. 421:** Examines the dilemma of **H.W. Muntinghe**, President of the High Court of Justice, who had pledged to Janssens not to render active service to the British while French forces still held out on the island. On 11 September 1811 at Molenvliet, Lord Minto [Gilbert Elliott, Lord Minto] provisionally retained Dutch courts and officials; the next day Muntinghe explained his scruples to Raffles [Thomas Stamford Raffles], who provisionally limited the High Court's duties to matters of immediate public peace and safety. *(Margin note: "conditions of capitulation at Kali Tuntang")* | **Page 421:** Examines High Court President **H.W. Muntinghe**'s moral dilemma on 11–12 September 1811 after Lord Minto's Molenvliet address, when Raffles provisionally narrowed the High Court's remit to immediate public peace so Muntinghe could reconcile duty with his pledge to Janssens. |
