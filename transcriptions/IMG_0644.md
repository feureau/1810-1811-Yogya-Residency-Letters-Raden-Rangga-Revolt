### Two-Column Transcription (Margin Notes / Javanese Script vs. Main Text / Dutch Translation)

| Margin Notes / Javanese Script | Main Text / Dutch Translation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Paulus to Eng.**<br>7 Dec. 1810<br>(Kraton of Madiun)<br><br>*Occupation of Madiun* | Lieutenant Paulus to Resident Pieter Engelhard [Eng.] (7 Dec. 1810, from the rebel palace at Madiun):<br>• Royal expeditionary forces formally occupy the fortified *kraton* / residence (*dalèm*) of Radèn Rongga Prawiradirja III [R. Rangga] on 6 Dec. 1810 following a grueling six-hour forced march.<br>• The palace compound is discovered wide open, abandoned, and empty.<br>• On 5 Dec., Mas Tumênggung Sumanêgara [SN] reportedly defects from Rangga and flees to Sasradiningrat at Rajêgwêsi; though a counter-report indicates Sumanêgara may still linger with Rangga. |
| **Rangga's Flight to Wilis**<br>c. 2 Dec. 1810 | Rebel evacuation into the highlands:<br>• Rangga fled Madiun around 2 Dec. 1810, accompanied by his elderly mother, children, and approximately 150 armed followers.<br>• Recognizing his force was far too weak to confront the royal Yogyakarta expedition in open battle, Rangga retreated toward the slopes of Mount Wilis (*Redi Wilis*), positioned on the mountainous frontier between Caruban and Madiun (two long days' march from Madiun). |
| **Regency Reinforcements**<br>Dec. 1810 | Purwadipura's tactical pause:<br>• Purwadipura [PWD] decides to halt and await the arrival of loyal Mancanagara [MN] regents before pursuing Rangga into the mountains:<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• Sasradipura and Sasrawinata (Magetan)<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• Pringgalaya (Kèrtasara)<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• Mangundirana (Kalangbrèt)<br>&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• Pringgakusuma (Rawa)<br>• **Severe logistical impediment:** Terrible monsoon weather, flooded dirt roads, and severe scarcity of provisions render rapid forced marches nearly impossible. |
| **Flying Column & Tollgates**<br>Dec. 1810<br><br>*Deserted Bandhars* | Pursuit operations and civilian devastation:<br>• Purwadipura prepares to send out a light flying column (*vliegende colonne*) under translator (*taalkundige*) Lucas Leberfeld and Radèn Tumênggung Kèrtalaya (noted: *Lanten*).<br>• Numerous villages (*desas*) and Chinese tollgates (*bandhars*) across the surrounding countryside are found entirely deserted out of terror of both the royal troops and Rangga's men.<br>• Purwadipura attempts to pacify and recall the local population: Many Javanese villagers cautiously return to Madiun, but the Chinese inhabitants remain absent.<br>• Tollgates of Ngawi and Magetan completely abandoned.<br>• **Ordnance & Loot:** Two 2½-lb artillery cannon recovered inside the *dalèm*; several other guns were spiked and cast into deep wells; palace furnishings left behind include European beds, stools, small tables, and tables. |
| **Paulus to Eng.**<br>8 Dec. 1810<br>(Maospati) | Lieutenant Paulus to Resident Engelhard (8 Dec. 1810, from Maospati):<br>• Expresses the extreme difficulty of placing full military reliance upon native auxiliary troops; resolves nevertheless to maintain trust in Purwadipura.<br>• Dispatches undercover reconnaissance scouts and spies to track Rangga's precise line of retreat into the Wilis ranges. |
