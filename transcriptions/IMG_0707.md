| **p. 33:** Most powerful Rajah, Karang Asem possesses Lombok, can muster 30,000 men; Rajah of Karang Asem 10,000.<br>Rajah of Badong earlier Dutch connections.<br>Idea of getting 15–20,000 men to co-operate + British troops from Bali / Lombok + whatever quantity of livestock, fresh provisions and live-stock (as) may be required for our armies in Java...<br>Holding Dutch in check in Surabaya by Balinese force if campaign prolonged.<br>Brit. influence via Bali to Bugis / Macassar tribes: Sumbawa / Bima / Endeh. | **p. 33:** The most powerful ruler, the Rajah of Karangasem (who also held Lombok), could muster 30,000 men (Karangasem alone 10,000), while the Rajah of Badung had earlier Dutch ties. Raffles envisaged 15,000–20,000 Balinese/Lombok troops cooperating with British forces alongside abundant livestock and provisions, holding the Dutch in check at Surabaya if the campaign dragged on, and extending British influence to Bugis and Makassarese groups in Sumbawa, Bima, and Ende. |
| **p. 34:** *"With respect to my communications with Madura, Solo and Mataram, though I can entertain little doubt that some of them have reached their destination, yet I have not been able to learn what effects they have produced, chiefly from the causes to which I have already alluded as increasing the natural difficulty of communication with those states. My Malay intelligence, however, states that a rumour prevails to the eastward that signs of disaffection and a refractory spirit have displayed themselves, not only in the island of Madura, but also in the interior of Java."* | **p. 34:** Direct quotation from Raffles noting that although his dispatches to Madura, Surakarta, and Mataram likely arrived, blockade difficulties prevented confirming their effect; Malay intelligence reported rumors eastward of disaffection and a refractory spirit in both Madura and interior Java. |
| **p. 37:** Idea of Dutch carrying on resistance from Bagelèn / Sukapura N. coast of Java.<br>*(Margin note: `*` retreat to the eastern division of the island)*<br>*"This suggests the obvious propriety of endeavouring as soon as possible to establish an intercourse between the English and the two principal chiefs of the interior of Java, the Susuhunan of Solo, or Carta Sura, and the Sultan of Mataram without the permission or rather the co-operation of whom, it would be impossible either for the Marshal's [Daendels] army either to take post amid the mountains, or by that way [to retreat to the eastern division]."* | **p. 37:** Anticipating Dutch resistance retreating into Bagelèn/Sukapura or the eastern division, Raffles emphasized the urgent need to establish direct British relations with Java's two principal interior rulers—the Susuhunan of Surakarta and the Sultan of Mataram—without whose cooperation Marshal Daendels's army could neither hold the mountains nor retreat eastward. |
